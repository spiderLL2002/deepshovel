# DeepShovel Product Context

## Product

DeepShovel is a scientific evidence workspace. It turns uploaded papers into traceable claims, observations, source anchors, citation reception, and claim-to-claim relationships without hiding the original material.

## Primary users

- Laboratory researchers validating a scientific proposition against an internal paper corpus.
- Reviewers auditing whether a claim is supported by the cited passage, experiment, figure, or table.
- Research leads comparing support, qualification, contradiction, and later-paper confirmation across papers.

Users often work for long sessions in bright office or laboratory environments. They value density, stable navigation, exact provenance, and honest uncertainty more than decorative novelty.

## Core jobs

1. Search a research proposition and understand why each result is related.
2. Upload, compile, version, and remove source material safely.
3. Inspect a paper's evidence path from claim to observation to exact source anchor.
4. See how later papers affirm, question, qualify, or contradict a cited result.
5. Explore OpenAlex taxonomy and one-hop claim relationships.
6. Record human review without weakening immutable machine evidence.

## Brand character

- 实验仪器般精密
- 编辑台般清晰
- 档案系统般可信

The interface should feel calm, technical, and deliberate. Its confidence comes from structure, provenance, and restraint.

## Visual references

- Scientific journals: readable hierarchy, disciplined tables, and clear captions.
- Research instruments: compact controls, high legibility, and unambiguous states.
- Professional editorial tools: stable navigation and content-first layouts.

## Anti-references

- Black-and-gold or neon "sci-fi" dashboards.
- Glassmorphism, gradient text, glowing nodes, decorative metrics, and AI sparkle motifs.
- Oversized dashed empty states, generic card grids, excessive pills, or every section floating in a shadow.
- Marketing copy that foregrounds the model instead of the research task.

## Product principles

- Preserve the distinction between what a paper says, what its own evidence supports, and what later literature says about it.
- Never imply that a whole paper is correct when the evidence only concerns one result or experiment.
- Keep source anchors and uncertainty visible near every consequential judgment.
- Let partial services fail locally; search, paper detail, and source evidence must remain usable.
- Prefer progressive disclosure for dense scientific detail, without hiding primary actions or current status.
